How Emerging Markets Are Reshaping Global Investment Strategies

G2G · August 29, 2026 · 5 min read

A spread is the price of getting in and out, and it moves for reasons that have nothing to do with the headline you just read. Three forces do most of the work: how much liquidity is sitting on the book, which sessions are open, and how much event risk the market is carrying into the next hour.

Liquidity is the floor

The tightest spread you will ever see on an instrument is set by how many participants are quoting it. On a major pair during the London session that number is large and the floor is low. On a minor pair at 03:00 server time it is not, and no amount of calm in the news cycle will change that.

If you only ever check the spread at one time of day, you are not comparing brokers — you are comparing sessions.

Session overlap is the multiplier

London and New York overlap for roughly four hours. During that window two of the deepest pools of liquidity are quoting the same instruments at the same time, and spreads on the majors compress accordingly. The reverse is also true — the handover between New York and Sydney is the thinnest part of the day.

Event risk is the spike

Ahead of a scheduled release, market makers widen because they cannot price the next print. This is the only one of the three that behaves like the popular story about news — and it resolves within minutes of the number landing, not hours.
